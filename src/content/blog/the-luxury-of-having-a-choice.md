Financial condition is more a mental condition than a physical one. Suppose a rich man and a struggling middle-class man are both travelling on a public bus; they have the same physical state, but it is the mental state that distinguishes them.

For the rich man, it’s a choice to travel by a crowded public bus; if he wants, he can travel by car too. But the other doesn’t have the same choice; it’s his only option to commute daily without spending beyond his limits. He has several other burdens and responsibilities to think of – how will he provide the rent for the month, or how can he save a few extras on groceries? He sinks himself into believing that he isn’t providing enough for his family as he wanted to. He ponders on the possibility of getting fired from his job, which he is led into due to the immense weight of responsibilities, and is reminded of thousands of other things that revolve in his mind. So, when a rich guy says, “I have been through a lot of struggles in my life,” what they basically mean is that they didn’t have Smoked Salmon Toast with Greek yogurt, eggs, nuts, and seed muesli for breakfast, or that they didn’t get much sleep due to an assignment, and that they aren’t having much of their daily meals since they are busy with their work schedule, even though they look prim, well-fed, and well-rested. So, when they say that they have a lot on their plate and its tough for them too, just remember that their ‘struggle’ is that someone else has bought a Roll Royce and they are still driving a BMW. I was in my first year of college, and I used to cycle 21–25 km daily to go to college and return home. Coming from a well-provided family, I used to consider myself a struggling, hardworking student until I heard the story of a classmate who travelled 50 km in a crowded, packed public bus, which took 2.5 hours for him to reach college. Another one first cycled to the bus stand, then took the bus to a railway station, and then again, a bus to reach college. This all humbled me to my core.

![the-luxury-of-having-a-choice](/uploads/the-luxury-of-having-a-choice.jpg)

The financial condition you have been brought up with from childhood till date defines your confidence and personality. Financial condition makes sure how you are developed, how you talk, how you interact, and how you present yourself to others. It automatically opens up hundreds of opportunities to choose from. That is exactly why the confidence and talking manner of a kid coming from an affluent family are extensively different from that of someone from a lower-middle-class family. The other kid doesn’t lack anything, to be honest. If he or she is less than the rich kid, it’s because the other kid never got any exposure; they never had better options to opt for. 

When a middle-class man buys a new phone or a new bike, they are never truly satisfied with it. Maybe they get around 2 seconds of happiness, and then again, the burden, responsibilities, and duties flood their mind. The anxiety of paying back the EMI weighs on them. A person is just one big medical bill or a medical crisis away from being completely broke.

**Being rich isn’t a bad thing, but one must never downplay their privilege.**
