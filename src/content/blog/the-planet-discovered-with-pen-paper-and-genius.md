A planet which was not discovered by any telescope

But was simply discovered with a pen and paper and sheer excellence of mathematical genius.

Roughly around 4.5 billion km from ***Sun***, the eighth planet of the solar system was not discovered by any telescopes or modern technology. In the 19th century astronomers observed a weird anomaly in the orbit of ***Uranus***; the seventh planet of the solar system discovered by ***William Herschel*** in 1781 using a telescope he built, it is supposed to have a predictable orbit according to Newton’s ***Universal Law of Gravitation*** as published in 1687.But ice giant wasn’t behaving as it was supposed to, at times, it would speed up, only to slow down considerably moments later. A fraction of the scientific community began discussing the possibility that Newton’s laws had failed. But amid all these speculations emerged the French mathematician and astronomer ***Urbain Le Verrier*.** He proposed that neither Newton’s laws nor Uranus’s orbit was wrong. The fault, he argued, lay with an unseen *gravitational phantom*—a mysterious celestial body whose gravitational influence was disturbing Uranus’s orbit. That very cosmic phantom would become the eighth planet of the Solar System: ***Neptune.*** But now came the conundrum: how could they determine the position of this *gravitational phantom*? To solve the problem, Le Verrier had to confront the ***three-body problem***—the Sun, Uranus, and the “mysterious celestial body.” These three celestial bodies exert gravitational forces on one another, influencing Uranus’s unusual orbit. Newton’s laws of gravitation help predict the orbit of a planet when it is influenced primarily by another body, but in this case, the usual two-body approach wasn’t sufficient. Le Verrier thought of applying his own perturbation function in the law of gravitation and with its help create a mathematical equation

> **d²r/dt² = -G \* M_Sun \* (r / r³)+ ∇R**

![the-planet-discovered-with-pen-paper-and-genius](/uploads/the-planet-discovered-with-pen-paper-and-genius.jpg)

Now this is pure genius of Le Verrier, the ∇R we see in this equation is *the gradient of the disturbing function (or perturbative function) R, representing forces like third-body gravitational attraction from other planets* and etc. This ∇R helps to determine everything about this unknown mystic gravitational phantom, although this equation wasn’t precise but after hours turned days and days to months and to years of crunching the equation and calculation brutally without any modern computer or any calculator with just raw brain power on **September 23, 1846,**using all the data and equation; ***Urbain Le Verrier*** finally finds out the coordinates of that gravitational phantom. Just think of the sheer brilliance of this man, that after this to confirm its existence he writes a letter to ***Berlin Observatory***, and an astronomer named ***Johann Gottfried Galle***  when he looked up the sky with the telescope under an hour and within an error of one degree, he becomes the first who saw ***Neptune*** through a telescope using the coordinates sent to him by Le Verrier. This entirely proves that with immense dedication, love, resilience and brilliance a human being can even discover a planet 4.5 billion km from Sun with just pen and paper.

Here’s some Wiki facts for you:\
*Neptune is the 8th and farthest known planet orbiting the Sun. It is the 4th largest planet in the Solar System by diameter, the 3rd most-massive planet, and the densest giant planet. It is 17 times the mass of Earth. Compared to Uranus, its neighboring ice giant, Neptune is slightly smaller, but more massive and dense. Being composed primarily of gases and liquids, it has no well-defined solid surface. Neptune orbits the Sun once every 164.8 years at an orbital distance of 30.1 astronomical units (4.5 billion kilometers). It is named after the Roman god of the sea and has the astronomical symbol ♆,representing Neptune's trident*
